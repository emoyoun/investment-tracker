# Weekly brief — 2026-09-07

Posture: the yen carry unwind is the live setup but credit is the live signal.
USD/JPY broke 155 to a six-month high past the intervention peak with a BOJ
hike ~97% priced for 9/18 — yet VIX 14.5, HY 2.65% and the S&P near highs mean
no cross-asset confirmation, so tw6 holds amber rather than going red. The real
change this week is one rung down the credit stack: **BB↔CCC compression has
reversed**. Meanwhile AI-infra credit stopped widening, which flips the ladder
overlay. What needs you: one new credit amber, two queued de-escalations, and
the 9/09 buyback test.

- **NEW: cr-ccc-oas promoted clear→amber — the BB↔CCC reversal is confirmed.**
  This is the week's most important read. CCC OAS is
  [10.51%](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/BAMLH0A3HYC) and sitting at its
  2026 wide, against **8.14% a year ago**, while BB OAS is
  [1.52%](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/BAMLH0A1HYBB) — essentially its
  2026 tight and inside last year's 1.86%. That is ~271bp of tier
  decompression in twelve months with IG flat year-over-year. The FT reported
  9/05 that CCC-and-lower is at its widest since the 2025 tariff shock (~15%
  all-in yields), with default-related actions $40.1bn YTD and recovery rates
  at 29% versus a 40% long-run average
  ([summary](https://en.sedaily.com/international/2026/09/07/junk-borrowing-costs-hit-highest-since-trump-tariff-shock)).
  Not red — IG is unchanged and the 12% backstop is far off — but this is the
  late-cycle turn tell firing, and it is the first tier to move.

- **TWO DE-ESCALATIONS QUEUED — cr-ai-infra → clear and tw5 → clear.** Both
  need your confirm or dismiss; both statuses stay amber until you accept. The
  AI-credit widening thesis was contradicted, not confirmed: Lambda closed a
  $926M GPU-collateralised TLB on 8/27 rated **Moody's Baa2 at SOFR+300**
  ([Lambda](https://lambda.ai/blog/lambda-closes-926-million-senior-secured-term-loan-b-facility)),
  which is **250bp inside CoreWeave's July SOFR+550** for similar collateral —
  the market is discriminating by offtaker credit, not repricing GPU paper
  wider. AI-infra equities rallied 5–20% on the week. On tw5, IREN filed
  **nothing** 8/31–9/07 and its marginal funding dollar has moved off equity to
  investment-grade GPU debt at 6.0% plus customer prepayments covering 45–55%
  of GPU capex
  ([FY26 release](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1878848/000187884826000051/irenreportsfy26results.htm)).
  Two corrections to standing premises: S&P cut Oracle to BBB− on **7/09**, not
  late August, and the ~215bp/~855bp CDS levels this dashboard carried are
  stale July prints from weak aggregators — treat them as unverified.

- **LADDER OVERLAY FLIPPED, and semis rallied away from the next rung.** SOXX
  closed [519.86](https://finance.yahoo.com/quote/SOXX/) on 9/04 = −20.8% from
  $656; the SOX rose 3.37% Friday with all 30 constituents up. The "don't add"
  overlay rested on AI-infra spreads staying wide and they did not, so the
  semis weakness now looks more mechanical than fundamental. Two remaining
  cautions before adding: the CCC tail above, and the fact that the new AI
  stress signals moved to the **demand** side — token prices broke below
  **$1/M tokens** for the first time (97 cents on 8/31, more than half off the
  summer high) and TSMC reportedly **delayed its 2027 advanced-packaging
  orders** ([CNBC 9/01](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/09/01/ai-token-prices-lows.html)).
  Goldman's "more demand plus lower prices is not automatically a positive" is
  the sharpest capex-ROI critique of the cycle.

- **tw7 disorderly watch is now LIVE, and 9/09 is the direct test.** The 10Y
  closed at or above the 4.75% line for **four consecutive sessions**
  ([FRED](https://fred.stlouisfed.org/series/DGS10)) with the 30Y at 5.28% near
  a 19-year high. Treasury's **doubled buybacks begin 9/09** — 10–20y and
  20–30y lifted from $2bn to at least $4bn per operation through 11/04
  ([Treasury](https://home.treasury.gov/news/press-releases/sb0607)). Yields
  rising *through* the buybacks is suppression failing; that is the thing to
  watch this week. The **proposed amber is still queued** on WALCL
  ([$6.737T, +$135B y/y](https://www.federalreserve.gov/releases/h41/20260903/))
  reading as non-crisis QE — but note it weakened: growth stalled (−$11.4bn
  over four weeks) and reserve balances actually *fell* $30bn as the TGA
  absorbs cash. Separately the SLR trigger is now affirmatively off the table —
  the final eSLR rule explicitly **declined** the Treasury exclusion.

- **chiptariff → proposed amber. Closest watch item to firing.** Commerce
  Secretary Lutnick confirmed the structure **on the record 9/02** — laptops,
  consoles and datacenter servers, with duty-free volumes capped in proportion
  to committed US production
  ([Bloomberg Law](https://news.bloomberglaw.com/international-trade/trump-to-levy-more-chip-tariffs-exemptions-for-us-manufacturing)).
  But a Federal Register check returns **zero** Phase-2 documents: no rate, no
  product list, no effective date, so it stays a watch. When it lands it will
  be durable — the 2/20/26 *Learning Resources* ruling left Section 232 as the
  surviving vehicle and 232 duties do not sunset. Helps US-committed fabs
  (TSMC-AZ, MU); hurts import-dependent designers, since relief scales to fabs
  you own.

- **Yen: no confirmation yet, but the fuel is still in the tank.** USD/JPY went
  160 → 154.06, the strongest yen since February and **through the level the
  joint intervention itself achieved**
  ([Japan Times](https://www.japantimes.co.jp/business/2026/09/07/markets/japan-yen-gains-154/)).
  Why it is not spent: CFTC data for the week to 8/25 had leveraged funds net
  short **81,619** yen contracts, so most of the short base has yet to cover.
  Washington has switched tack — Bessent now calls the moves *not* disorderly
  and wants BOJ hikes instead of intervention
  ([Reuters 9/01](https://www.reuters.com/world/asia-pacific/bessent-meets-bojs-ueda-calls-sound-policy-avoid-currency-volatility-treasury-2026-09-01/)).
  MoF's actual spend was far larger than this dashboard assumed: a record
  **¥15.4tn (~$98bn) between 7/30 and 8/26**. Calendar that resolves it: CPI
  **9/11**, FOMC **9/15–16** (50–64% hike odds), BOJ **9/18**.
